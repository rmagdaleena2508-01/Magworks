CubeSight

Point your camera at a scrambled Rubik's Cube and get a near-optimal solution, walked move-by-move on an interactive 3D cube — running entirely in the browser, with no app, no backend, and no build step.

Live site Code
Live · runs fully in the browser — camera frames never leave your device

Why it exists

I had a cube sitting scrambled on my desk for weeks. I went down the YouTube-tutorial rabbit hole trying to learn it by hand, got lost in algorithm notation, and decided to build the thing instead of memorising 40 cases I'd forget by next week.

What it does

Scan — hold your cube up to the camera, once per face. A grid overlay guides placement and a live per-cell colour readout shows what the camera sees before you commit to a capture.

Review — every detected sticker renders as an unfolded cube net. Anything the classifier wasn't sure about gets an amber ring; tap any sticker to cycle its colour by hand.

Solve — the app computes a solution (usually 18–22 moves) and renders it as an interactive 3D cube. Step through move-by-move, auto-play the whole thing, or scrub backward with full inverse-move undo. No sign-in, and the camera frames never leave the device.

The hard part: reading colour off a webcam

Solving a cube is a solved problem — cubejs ships Kociemba's two-phase algorithm as a library. The genuinely hard part is reading 54 stickers off a webcam accurately: auto white-balance shifts colours between captures, warm indoor light pushes orange toward red, glare washes stickers toward white, and cheap cubes have colour pairs (orange/red especially) that sit close together in raw RGB.

  • Per-sticker RGB didn't work. Classifying each sticker independently by nearest-centre RGB distance was too fragile — a couple of misclassifications per scan, always the same pairs (orange↔red, white↔yellow under warm light).
  • Work in CIELAB, not RGB. Convert every sample to CIELAB and weight the lightness channel down relative to a/b. Auto-exposure moves lightness around a lot between frames; the hue/chroma (a, b) is where the real colour signal lives.
  • Treat it as a balanced assignment, not 54 lookups. A cube has exactly 9 stickers of each colour, so solve for the assignment that respects that constraint. Centre stickers pin their face's colour (they never move on a 3×3), then a greedy min-distance matcher fills the other 48 slots, capped at 9 per colour.

How it's built

Five files, no bundler, nothing to install to run it. A Web Worker owns the Kociemba solver, the structural validity checks and the scan auto-repair; everything talks to it over postMessage RPC, so the solver's table generation and search never touch the main thread and the UI stays responsive while it computes.

Three.js builds the 26 cubies from a 54-character facelet string and animates the layer turns. The whole pipeline is: camera frame → sample 9 cells (median of 3 frames) → CIELAB balanced-assignment classification → facelet string → validity check (and auto-repair) → Kociemba solve → moves animated on the 3D cube.

A closer look

The review net — low-confidence stickers get an amber ring, centres are locked, and the legend tracks that each colour hits exactly 9 before you solve.
